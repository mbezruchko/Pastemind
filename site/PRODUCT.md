# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Anyone with a Mac (macOS 14+, Apple silicon), not only developers: people who copy things all day (links, addresses,
codes, screenshots, files) and lose them. They arrive from a social post (Threads), curious, and decide within seconds
whether the app is clever enough to install. Many work in more than one language.

## Product Purpose

The site explains and distributes Pastemind, a free, open-source (MIT) menu bar app: clipboard history you search by
meaning ("when does my plane leave" finds the flight confirmation), that also understands pictures, voice memos and
scanned PDFs, plus a search for apps, settings and files by what's in them. Everything runs on the Mac, with a local
model (EmbeddingGemma 2). Success: a visitor gets the "oh, it understood me" moment on the page, trusts that nothing
leaves their Mac, and downloads the notarized DMG.

## Brand Personality

Smart, playful, fast. Confident without shouting; a little wit in the copy; demos instead of claims. The emotional
goal is delight at being understood, then calm about privacy.

## Anti-references

- Stock illustrations: abstract people, 3D blobs, generic "AI" imagery in place of the real interface.
- Template SaaS landing pages: gradient hero, identical icon cards, "Trusted by" rows, hero metrics.
- Neon-on-black "AI tool" looks.

## Design Principles

- Show the real app. Every claim is backed by the actual interface or a live demo, never an illustration.
- Make the visitor feel understood: demonstrate search by meaning with real queries, in more than one language.
- Apple-product-page clarity: one idea per screen, short strong lines, the product large and crisp.
- Privacy is a feature, stated plainly with the specifics (local model, no account, open source, what goes online).
- Fast to load and fast to read: no tracking scripts, self-hosted fonts, light pages.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, keyboard access, visible focus, captions/alt text for all product imagery. Honor
prefers-reduced-motion (demos become static). The video plays muted and never carries information the text lacks.
